# 1. Project Overview

## 1.1 Product concept

English Conversation Partner is a conversation-first English learning
platform. Users learn English by exploring, listening to, understanding,
and practising realistic conversations from everyday life and
professional situations.

The product should help users answer practical questions such as:

-   What should I say when I visit a shop?
-   How do I submit a file to my manager?
-   How do I speak with a school guardian?
-   What should I say at an airport, hotel, bank, or hospital?
-   How can I disagree, argue, complain, apologise, negotiate, or
    resolve a misunderstanding?
-   How does a lawyer communicate with a client?
-   How does a doctor explain something to a patient?
-   How does a freelancer discuss project scope, deadlines, and feedback
    with a client?

The platform is not intended to be a traditional grammar-first course.
Its core learning sequence is:

**Real-life situation → Conversation → Listening → Translation →
Vocabulary → Contextual Grammar → Speaking Practice → Feedback →
Revision**

The product should support complete beginners through advanced learners,
as well as people from a wide range of professions.

## 1.2 Product vision

Make it possible for a learner to prepare for almost any real-life or
professional interaction in English.

The product should help learners understand: 1. What to say. 2. When to
say it. 3. How to say it naturally. 4. How to understand the other
person's response. 5. How to respond appropriately. 6. How to improve
their English after practising.

## 1.3 Core product principles

1.  **Conversation-first:** Conversations are the primary learning unit.
    Grammar and vocabulary support the conversation rather than replace
    it.
2.  **Practical and realistic:** Dialogue should sound like natural
    human communication, not textbook examples.
3.  **Beginner-friendly, not beginner-limited:** Every suitable scenario
    should offer a clear progression to more complex versions.
4.  **Context-aware learning:** Word meanings, translations, grammar
    explanations, and alternative expressions must reflect the actual
    sentence context.
5.  **Learn at multiple levels:** Support short, medium, and long
    conversations, as well as CEFR-inspired proficiency levels.
6.  **Listen and speak:** Audio and active practice are essential parts
    of the learning experience.
7.  **Reusable content:** One scenario can appear in multiple discovery
    categories without duplicating its underlying content.
8.  **Expandable architecture:** New categories, professions, languages,
    scenarios, translations, and learning activities should be addable
    without redesigning the application.

# 2. Target Users

## 2.1 Primary audience

-   People learning English for everyday communication.
-   Students and university applicants.
-   Job seekers and interview candidates.
-   Working professionals.
-   Freelancers and remote workers.
-   Business owners and entrepreneurs.
-   People who need English for travel, study, migration, or customer
    communication.
-   Professionals who need role-specific English.

## 2.2 Skill levels

Use CEFR as a reference framework:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Level                   Description             Learning expectation
  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
  A1                      Beginner                Very short, simple
                                                  exchanges

  A2                      Elementary              Familiar everyday
                                                  situations

  B1                      Intermediate            Longer conversations
                                                  and explanations

  B2                      Upper-intermediate      Discussion,
                                                  negotiation, and
                                                  problem-solving

  C1                      Advanced                Nuanced, complex, and
                                                  professional
                                                  communication

  C2                      Proficient              Highly flexible and
                                                  sophisticated
                                                  communication
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

CEFR labels should not be assigned casually. Conversation difficulty
should be based on sentence complexity, vocabulary, speed, implied
meaning, and communication demands.

## 2.3 User needs

Users need to: - Find a situation quickly. - Understand a conversation
without already knowing every word. - Listen to individual lines or the
full dialogue. - Read translations in their preferred language. -
explore unfamiliar vocabulary. - Open a contextual grammar explanation
when needed. - practise responding without simply memorising a script. -
save useful conversations and words. - see learning progress and revisit
previous material.

# 4. Conversation Content Structure

Every learning scenario should follow a consistent reusable structure.

## 4.1 Scenario metadata

Each scenario should include:

-   Scenario title
-   Short description
-   Main category
-   Subcategory
-   Additional categories and tags
-   Profession or job role, if relevant
-   Context and learning objective
-   CEFR-inspired level
-   Estimated learning time
-   Conversation length
-   Communication style: casual, neutral, formal
-   Participants / speaker roles
-   Prerequisite scenarios, if applicable
-   Published status

## 4.2 Conversation versions

A scenario may offer different versions:

  Version     Suggested length Purpose
  --------- ------------------ --------------------------------
  Short         4--6 exchanges Quick beginner practice
  Medium       8--12 exchanges More complete interaction
  Long        15--25 exchanges Detailed real-life interaction

These are starting guidelines, not rigid rules.

Where appropriate, offer: - Easy - Normal - Hard

Do not create artificial longer versions by repeating lines. Each
version should add meaningful conversational depth.

## 4.3 Conversation style

Where useful, provide: - Casual / Informal - Neutral / Everyday - Formal
/ Professional

The style must match the real situation. For example, a conversation
with a close friend should not sound like a legal letter.

## 4.4 Dialogue line data

Each dialogue line should support:

-   Speaker name / role
-   English sentence
-   Optional contextual translation
-   Sentence audio
-   Optional pronunciation support
-   Key vocabulary references
-   Optional grammar references
-   Optional learning note
-   Line order / sequence

## 4.5 Conversation-level learning materials

Each conversation may include:

1.  Full conversation audio
2.  Individual sentence audio
3.  Full conversation translation
4.  Sentence-by-sentence translation
5.  Vocabulary list
6.  Contextual grammar explanations
7.  Useful expressions
8.  Common mistakes
9.  Listening comprehension questions
10. Speaking / roleplay practice
11. Review activity

Do not require every possible material to exist before a scenario can be
published. The admin should be able to manage content completeness and
publication readiness.

# 5. Core Learning Experience

## 5.1 Recommended learning flow

**Discover → Understand → Listen → Explore → Practise → Review**

### Step 1: Discover

The user finds a conversation through: - Category - Profession -
Search - Difficulty - Conversation length - Communication style -
Recommended content

### Step 2: Understand the context

Show: - Scenario title - Short situation description - Who is speaking -
Learning objective - Level and estimated duration

### Step 3: Read the conversation

Present dialogue in a clear, readable layout.

Each line should have: - Speaker label - English sentence - Individual
audio control - Translation reveal / hide control - Optional word
interaction

Avoid showing too much secondary information by default. The learner
should be able to focus on the dialogue.

### Step 4: Listen

Support: - Full conversation playback - Individual sentence playback -
Play / pause - Replay - Playback speed - Speaker voice differentiation
where available - Optional American and British English voice variants

### Step 5: Explore vocabulary

Show important or potentially difficult words from the dialogue.

Each vocabulary item should support: - Word - Pronunciation - Part of
speech - Contextual meaning - Word forms where applicable - Synonyms -
Antonyms where useful - Example sentences - Audio - Save to vocabulary
notebook

Do not show irrelevant or forced linguistic properties. For example, not
every word has a meaningful antonym or multiple parts of speech.

### Step 6: Explore grammar

A "See Grammar" action opens a modal, drawer, or expandable panel.

Grammar should be contextual and progressive.

### Step 7: Practise

Allow the user to: - Read the conversation aloud - Practise one role -
Hide one speaker's lines and respond independently - Complete missing
lines - Answer comprehension questions - Start an AI roleplay when that
feature is available

### Step 8: Review

At the end, show: - Completed status - Words learned - Practice result,
if applicable - Suggested next conversation - Option to repeat or
bookmark

# 6. Grammar Explorer Specification

Grammar is a supporting feature, not the primary navigation or primary
course structure.

## 6.1 Word-level Grammar Explorer

When a learner clicks a vocabulary word, open a contextual panel.

Possible sections:

1.  Word and pronunciation
2.  Contextual meaning in the current sentence
3.  Part of Speech
4.  Base / root form
5.  Word forms
6.  Verb forms, if applicable
7.  Noun / adjective / adverb family, if applicable
8.  Singular and plural, if applicable
9.  Comparative and superlative, if applicable
10. Regular or irregular verb, if applicable
11. Transitive / intransitive usage, if applicable
12. Synonyms
13. Antonyms, where useful
14. Common collocations
15. Prefix and suffix, where useful
16. Example sentences
17. Common mistakes
18. Formal / informal usage
19. Audio pronunciation

## 6.2 Sentence-level Grammar

For a selected sentence, show: - Sentence meaning - Sentence structure -
Subject - Main verb - Object / complement, where applicable - Tense and
why it is used - Relevant tense pattern - Important grammatical
elements - Alternative natural sentence structures - Common learner
mistakes

## 6.3 Grammar accuracy requirements

-   Explain grammar in simple language.
-   Always use the actual sentence as context.
-   Distinguish grammatical form from contextual meaning.
-   Do not claim a word belongs to a part of speech that it does not
    have in that sentence.
-   Avoid displaying every possible grammar category for every word.
-   Allow beginner-friendly explanations first and advanced details on
    demand.
-   Do not invent grammatical rules or unsupported word forms.

# 7. Translation System

## 7.1 Language selection

The initial product should support English and Bangla particularly well.
Design the data model to support additional languages.

Potential future languages: - Hindi - Arabic - Urdu - Spanish - French -
German - Japanese - Chinese - Korean

The user should be able to choose a preferred translation language in
settings and change it while learning.

## 7.2 Translation modes

1.  Full conversation translation
2.  Sentence-by-sentence translation
3.  Word meaning
4.  Hide translation
5.  Optional phrase / expression explanation

## 7.3 Translation quality

-   Translate naturally, not mechanically.
-   Preserve context, tone, politeness, and intent.
-   Explain idioms and phrasal verbs by meaning.
-   Support regional differences when they materially affect meaning.
-   Keep source and target sentences clearly aligned.
-   Do not silently replace the original English sentence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Audio & Pronunciation System

Audio should be reusable and independently manageable.

## 8.1 Audio types

-   Full conversation audio
-   Individual sentence audio
-   Individual word pronunciation audio
-   Optional phrase audio
-   Optional learner recording for speaking practice

## 8.2 Audio controls

-   Play / pause
-   Replay
-   Playback speed: 0.75x, 1x, 1.25x, 1.5x, 2x
-   Progress indicator for full audio
-   Speaker differentiation
-   Optional American English and British English variants

## 8.3 Audio implementation principles

-   Store audio references/URLs rather than embedding large audio files
    in content records.
-   Reuse the same audio asset when the same sentence and voice
    configuration are reused.
-   Generate audio through a suitable TTS provider or recorded assets.
-   Do not assume a provider or voice is available until it is verified.
-   Include loading, error, and retry states.
-   Make audio controls accessible by keyboard and screen reader.
-   Avoid automatically playing audio without user action.

# 9. AI Speaking Partner --- Planned Advanced Feature

This is a major differentiator, but it may be released after the core
conversation library.

## 9.1 Roleplay experience

The learner selects a scenario and chooses a role.

Example: - Scenario: At the Airport - AI role: Check-in staff - Learner
role: Passenger

The AI starts the conversation and responds naturally to the learner.

## 9.2 Behaviour

The AI should: - Adapt to the learner's selected level. - Stay within
the scenario unless the learner takes the conversation in a reasonable
direction. - Ask natural follow-up questions. - Avoid simply repeating a
fixed script. - Give hints when the learner is stuck. - Offer a
correction without interrupting every sentence. - Explain mistakes in a
supportive way. - Allow another attempt.

## 9.3 Feedback

After a practice session, provide: - Grammar accuracy - Vocabulary use -
Sentence formation - Naturalness of expression - Relevance of response -
Pronunciation feedback, only when reliable speech analysis is
available - Suggested natural alternatives - A short improvement plan

Do not present automated pronunciation or fluency scores as perfectly
objective. Explain limitations and provide actionable feedback.

## 9.4 Text and voice modes

Plan for: - Text roleplay - Voice roleplay - Session transcript -
Replay - Feedback summary

Voice roleplay requires a suitable speech recognition and audio
generation architecture. Keep it modular.

# 17. Technical & Engineering Principles

The user intends to build the product with Google Antigravity. The agent
must inspect the actual project workspace before making technology
decisions or changing files.

## 17.1 Technology selection

Do not assume a framework, database, authentication provider, hosting
provider, TTS provider, or AI provider without inspecting the existing
workspace and discussing significant decisions.

If the workspace is empty: 1. Recommend a suitable, maintainable web
stack. 2. Explain why it fits this product. 3. Identify likely hosting,
database, authentication, storage, and AI/audio costs. 4. Present the
proposed stack and wait for approval before scaffolding.

## 17.2 Engineering quality

-   Use reusable components.
-   Keep content separate from presentation.
-   Use typed data structures where supported.
-   Validate user input.
-   Handle loading, empty, and error states.
-   Use secure authentication and authorisation.
-   Protect admin routes and content-management actions.
-   Avoid exposing secret keys in client code.
-   Keep AI and audio provider integrations behind server-side services.
-   Avoid hardcoding large conversation datasets in UI components.
-   Add tests for important learning flows and data validation.
-   Document environment variables without committing secrets.
-   Keep the application responsive and accessible.
-   Use migrations or a controlled schema evolution process.
-   Avoid unnecessary dependencies and premature complexity.

## 17.3 Internationalisation

-   Store language codes consistently.
-   Keep English source content separate from translations.
-   Do not assume Bangla will always be the only translation language.
-   Support Unicode properly.
-   Design for text expansion and different scripts.
-   Ensure translated text does not break the dialogue layout.

## 17.4 Performance

-   Paginate or progressively load large conversation libraries.
-   Lazy-load non-critical media.
-   Cache reusable content appropriately.
-   Avoid loading every audio asset on initial page load.
-   Optimise images and audio delivery.
-   Keep the initial learning page fast.

# 20. Product Risks & Decisions to Resolve

1.  **Content volume:** Thousands of conversations require a content
    production and review workflow. Do not generate large quantities of
    unreviewed content.
2.  **Translation accuracy:** Machine translations need review,
    particularly for idioms and professional terminology.
3.  **Grammar accuracy:** Grammar data must be context-aware and
    validated.
4.  **Audio cost:** Sentence-level and multi-accent audio can become
    expensive. Reuse generated assets and monitor usage.
5.  **AI cost:** Voice roleplay may have substantial per-session cost.
    Introduce usage limits or plans only after validating demand.
6.  **Professional reliability:** Legal, medical, financial, and other
    specialist content should be reviewed by knowledgeable people and
    should not be presented as professional advice.
7.  **Scope creep:** Keep the first release focused on the core learning
    loop.
8.  **CEFR alignment:** Use CEFR as a guide and validate level
    assignments rather than treating labels as decoration.

# 23. Final Product Success Criteria

The product is successful when a learner can:

1.  Find a real-life situation they want to prepare for.
2.  Choose a suitable difficulty and conversation length.
3.  Understand who is speaking and why.
4.  Read the English conversation naturally.
5.  Listen to the complete dialogue or a specific sentence.
6.  Reveal a useful translation.
7.  Understand unfamiliar words in context.
8.  Explore grammar without leaving the learning flow.
9.  Practise responding independently.
10. Save and revisit useful learning materials.
11. See meaningful progress over time.

**The most important principle: Do not build a large collection of
disconnected English lessons. Build one reusable learning system that
can turn any real-life or professional situation into a complete
conversation-based English learning experience.**
